# Menstrual Cycle Phases, Hormonal Baselines & Fertility Awareness

## Journey: Cycle & Fertility
**Category:** menstrual_cycle / ovulation_awareness / hormonal_patterns

### The Four Phases of the Female Cycle
The menstrual cycle is a vital sign reflecting overall endocrine balance. It consists of four distinct phases:
1. **Menstrual Phase (Days 1–5):** Estrogen and progesterone are at baseline. The endometrium sheds. Rest, iron-rich nutrition, and hydration support physical replenishment.
2. **Follicular Phase (Days 6–13):** Follicle-Stimulating Hormone (FSH) stimulates ovarian follicle maturation. Rising 17-beta estradiol enhances energy, insulin sensitivity, and positive mood.
3. **Ovulatory Phase (Around Day 14 in an average 28-day cycle):** A surge in Luteinizing Hormone (LH) triggers mature oocyte release. The fertile window spans approximately 5 days prior to ovulation plus the day of ovulation.
4. **Luteal Phase (Days 15–28):** The corpus luteum secretes progesterone, shifting basal metabolic rate slightly upward (+100–300 kcal/day) and raising basal body temperature by 0.3°C to 0.5°C.

### Basal Body Temperature and Cervical Fluid Tracking
Tracking Basal Body Temperature (BBT) requires measuring temperature immediately upon waking before getting out of bed. A sustained thermal shift confirms that ovulation has occurred. Leading up to ovulation, rising estrogen causes cervical fluid to transition from dry/sticky to clear, stretchy, egg-white consistency (peak fertility indicator).

### Supporting Luteal Phase Progesterone Naturally
Progesterone exerts a calming neurosteroid effect via allopregnanolone on GABA receptors. Supporting adequate luteal phase length (typically 11–14 days) involves maintaining healthy vitamin B6, magnesium glycinate, zinc, and reducing chronic sympathetic nervous system activation, which can siphon steroidogenic precursors away from progesterone production.
